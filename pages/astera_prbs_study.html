AI-Generated PRBS Generator RTL Study

As part of an initiative to explore the capabilities of Large Language Models in hardware design, I conducted a study comparing fully AI-generated RTL against Astera Labs' existing production IP. The focus of the study was a Pseudo-Random Bit Sequence (PRBS) Generator targeting the TSMC N3 process node at 1.75 GHz (256-bit).
Synthesized area comparison between human reference and AI-generated RTL
Study Methodology
The study involved building serial, parallel, and multi-polynomial generators (e.g., PRBS-7, PRBS-13, PRBS-31). For the final task, the AI was prompted to write the RTL from the specification alone, without seeing any reference code. We then synthesized this AI-generated RTL against the human-written production reference and another AI variant that had seen the reference code.

Key Takeaways & Findings

  • Spec-only RTL matched production: The AI successfully generated bit-exact RTL across 26K+ vectors and met timing at 1.75 GHz. Interestingly, allowing the AI to see the human reference code provided zero synthesis advantage.
  • Synthesis erases coding style: While human readability choices drastically affect the source code's look, the synthesis tool collapsed the three different RTL styles into identical logic gates. Readability choices did not affect Power, Performance, or Area (PPA).
  • Architectural Gaps: The main difference lay in module boundary definitions. The AI tended to fuse logic into a single monolithic module, whereas the human designer split it into logical submodules, allowing the synthesis tool to optimize across boundaries. For PRBS-31 + Gray encoding, this architectural decision cost the AI-generated design +25% in area (478 vs 390 cells).
