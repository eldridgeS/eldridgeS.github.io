As part of my broader contributions to the verification team at Astera Labs, I was tasked with verifying the APB Clock Domain Crossing (CDC) Bridge. This project presented a unique challenge: verifying a "moving target" where the RTL design was actively evolving while I was building the verification infrastructure.
UVM Verification Environment for the APB CDC Bridge
Verifying a Moving Target (Hornet)
Clock Domain Crossing bridges are notoriously tricky due to the asynchronous nature of the interfaces. The design team was actively reworking the architecture to improve latency and robustness, which meant the verification environment had to adapt rapidly to fundamental architectural shifts.

Verification Journey

  • Baseline Verification: I initially verified the baseline design, bringing up 11 UVM tests and proving 22 formal assertions exhaustively. In this phase, the watchdog timer lived in the destination domain.
  • Design Rework: Mid-project, the RTL was reworked. The watchdog was moved to the source domain, and new features like a stale-response interlock and FIFO-state timeout were introduced.
  • Agile Adaptation: I actively iterated on the UVM environment, updating sequencers, drivers, and the central scoreboard to handle the new address/data/strobe response paths. Simultaneously, I rewrote the formal SystemVerilog Assertions (SVA) in step with each RTL change to ensure continuous proof of correctness.
Key Takeaways
This project honed my ability to work closely with RTL designers in a fast-paced environment. It demonstrated that a strong, modular UVM and Formal setup is essential not just for proving a static design, but for safely supporting rapid architectural refactoring without introducing regression bugs.
